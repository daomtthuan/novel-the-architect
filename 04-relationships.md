# Relationship Map (v7, World Calendar canon)

## The Architect ↔ Elena

Core relationship of Season 1.

Progression:

1. strangers (WC15)
2. companions
3. the one who breaks his Darkest Point ideology (WC23)
4. love
5. marriage (WC24)
6. family (Son born WC35)
7. shared civilization
8. sacrifice (WC35)
9. grief and legacy

Include ordinary domestic scenes, humor, arguments, vulnerability, and mutual respect.

Central principle:

> He loves her enough to want to save her, but respects her enough to accept her choice. That single act of acceptance is what makes him The Architect rather than merely a Builder.

## The Architect ↔ Father and Mother (new, v7)

Loving, ordinary parents — retired A-rank adventurers who chose an ordinary life after marrying. The Father requests The Teacher take his son on as a student (WC9). Both die at WC16, in the same battle as The Teacher and the Scout, saving the Architect from a high-tier Demon. His father's last act is to physically throw him clear of the finishing blow. This loss — not The Teacher's alone — is the true origin of the Darkest Point.

## The Architect ↔ The Teacher

Mentor → prodigy. Formerly the mage of the same disbanded party as the Architect's parents — not an unconnected mentor figure. Ends at WC16 with the Teacher's death, which the Architect caused through his own tactical error, alongside the deaths of his parents and the Scout. This relationship, combined with the loss of his parents, is the origin of the Darkest Point.

## The Architect ↔ The Guild Master (new, v7)

The sole adult survivor of the original five-person party (absent from the WC16 battle, coordinating the guild's response). Becomes a recurring political and material supporter of the Architect from WC16 onward, through The Guild.

## The Architect ↔ Iris

Childhood-onward friendship (Iris introduced at 12, strictly platonic at that stage) → quiet, unspoken attraction on Iris's side once she is an adult (~WC21–24) → her own choice to step back when he marries Elena → trusted, permanent found-family → after WC35, co-parent of the Son and his sword teacher.

## The Architect ↔ Seraphine

Enemy infiltrator/seductress (WC33) → jealousy trigger for Elena → witness to the war's true cost → defector (WC35) → guilt-driven, permanently refused would-be lover, granted only the small concession of a nickname ("Sera," at her own request, marking that she is no longer an enemy) → falls genuinely in love and loses her demonic power as a result → co-parent of the Son (magic teacher).

## The Architect ↔ Isolde

Political marriage proposal from the Empire of Veyrand (WC26) → refusal → political ally → wartime ally → a refusal that persists even once Elena herself would permit a second marriage, establishing it as the Architect's own principle.

## Secondary Romantic Tension (summary)

Iris, Seraphine, and Isolde each develop real attraction toward the Architect. Their feelings create comedy, jealousy, politics, friendship, rivalry, admiration, and character development. This is a light harem atmosphere, not a conventional harem structure — none of them is ever accepted as an equal or replacement partner.

## Architect ↔ Son

Protective father → grief-shadowed distance → the Son's ideological questioning → The Break (WC67) → investigation → reconciliation (WC79) → a short, real family period (WC80) → the Architect's death (WC82), which lands as the same beat as full reconciliation.

The son can love him while condemning his decisions along the way.

## Elena ↔ Son

They never meet — Elena dies the day he is born. Their relationship survives entirely through memories, Iris's and Seraphine's stories, records, objects, and letters. Her motherhood remains emotionally present despite the absence.

## Iris ↔ Son / Seraphine ↔ Son

Both women become primary caretakers after WC35. Iris trains him in swordsmanship (playful from ~WC42, formal from ~WC43); Seraphine teaches him magic (from ~WC48). Their combined presence keeps the Son from growing up with only his grieving father as an emotional anchor.

## Architect ↔ The Other Builder

They are mirrors, from two different timelines. Both loved an Elena. Both built civilizations. Both believe they protect people.

Difference: at the defining choice, the primary Architect accepted imperfect freedom — Elena's right to choose. The Other Builder refused that freedom for his own Elena, saved her by force, and never became an Architect as a result; he remains a Builder, arrested at his own version of the Darkest Point.

## Mira ↔ Architect

Historian → skeptic → investigator → understanding. Mira eventually stops seeing a legend and sees a flawed human who once made one very costly mistake (First Major Loss, WC16) and one very difficult right choice (Elena, WC35).

## Mira ↔ Elena

Mira first treats Elena as historical evidence. Eventually she understands Elena as a person, and as a cross-timeline Quantum Anchor phenomenon.

## Noah ↔ Architect

Noah knows him only through history. The relationship is inherited rather than biological, and Noah is explicitly not a Builder.

## Noah ↔ Real-world Alternate Architect / Builder

No blood or direct personal relationship. The connection is cross-timeline resonance. An older, ordinary alternate Builder sees Elena's image and experiences an unexplained emotional reaction.
