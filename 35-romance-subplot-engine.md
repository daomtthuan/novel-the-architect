# Romance Subplot Engine (v6, World Calendar canon)

## Purpose

The AI agent should automatically weave secondary romantic interactions (Iris, Seraphine, Isolde) into the main story without derailing the primary plot, and without ever placing romantic framing on Iris before she is an adult.

Romance is an **adaptive subplot layer**. The agent should not insert romance randomly.

## Scene Selection

A romance subplot may activate when the chapter contains: travel; camp/inn; academy or training life; festivals; diplomatic receptions; military preparation; research; domestic life; celebrations; recovery after battle; private conversations; (post-WC35) co-parenting scenes with the Son.

Avoid activating romance during scenes where it would destroy the intended emotional weight. Examples: The Teacher's death (WC16); Elena's death/Ark activation (WC35); the Architect's own death (WC82); any major mystery-board reveal in Movies 2–3.

## Subplot Activation Algorithm

Before writing a chapter:

1. Identify the main plot objective.
2. Identify which supporting characters are naturally present.
3. Check each character's current relationship state and computed age.
4. Select at most 1–2 romantic beats unless the chapter is explicitly romance-focused.
5. Make the beat arise naturally from the scene.
6. Ensure the main plot continues moving.
7. Update relationship memory (`42-romance-continuity-memory.md`).

## Types of Romantic Beats

### Micro Beat
One line or gesture — e.g., Iris quietly noticing the Architect is exhausted; Seraphine teasing him and getting no rise out of him.

### Comedy Beat
A misunderstanding or teasing exchange, most often Seraphine-driven.

### Emotional Beat
A character reveals genuine vulnerability — e.g., Seraphine's guilt; Iris's restraint; Isolde's relief at being refused for the right reasons.

### Relationship Beat
A bond changes — e.g., Iris's decision to step back at the wedding (WC24).

### Political Romance Beat
Isolde's arc is built almost entirely from this type.

### Foreshadowing Beat
A seemingly harmless interaction becomes meaningful later — e.g., Elena's jealousy of Seraphine (WC33) foreshadowing Seraphine's later guilt and devotion to the Son.

## Anti-Pattern

Never write:

> "The plot stopped because a beautiful girl appeared."

Instead:

> The character enters for a reason connected to the current story (a diplomatic mission, a battlefield assignment, a childhood friendship). Her romantic interaction should emerge from that reason.

## Relationship Progression

Do not jump: stranger → love interest without intermediate stages.

Use: stranger → acquaintance → trust → emotional familiarity → attraction → conflict / acceptance.

Iris specifically must show: child friend (WC15, platonic) → adult friend → quiet unspoken attraction (~WC21+) → deliberate withdrawal (WC24) → chosen family. Do not compress or skip the platonic childhood stage.

Not every character needs to reach the final stage.
