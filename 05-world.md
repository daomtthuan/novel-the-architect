# World Bible (v8, World Calendar canon)

## World Identity

A fantasy civilization with kingdoms, empires, city-states, villages, magical academies, churches, monster territories, demon civilizations, ancient ruins, magical infrastructure. Many nations exist — multiple Empires alongside multiple Kingdoms — with no single dominant hegemon; power is balanced and actively contested (see `06-factions-and-politics.md`).

The world should initially feel like fantasy, not disguised science fiction.

## Peoples

### Human

Standard 1.0× aging rate. The Architect and Iris are Human.

### Eterna

A long-lived, Light/Time-affinity people. Age at 0.25× the Human rate. Elena is Eterna. Their naming lineage explains existing artifact names — "Eterna Ring," "Eterna Bond" (wedding rings, see `24-artifact-registry.md` / `30-visual-bible.md`) — as Eterna-make heirloom pieces.

### Half-blood (Human × Eterna)

Age at 0.5× the Human rate. The Son is the setting's clearest example.

### Demon / Succubus

Varies by subtype; Succubi such as Seraphine are effectively ageless.

### Beastkin (v7)

Sovereign nation(s) of their own.

### Dwarf (v7)

Sovereign nation, mountain-dwelling, tied to magical/engineering infrastructure.

### Elf-adjacent / Sylvan (v7)

Stateless, reclusive, deep forests, distrustful of otherworlders.

### Undead / Revenant (v7)

Very rare, tied to Ark/Quantum-adjacent magic.

### Giant / Titan-kin (v7)

Ancient, near-extinct; a potential hook for "Other Builders across history" lore (see `08-multiverse-and-quantum-lore.md`).

## Civilization Stages

### Early

Magic is personal and localized.

### Architect Era (roughly Era 1–7, WC 0–82)

Magic becomes infrastructure: communication, transportation, energy, agriculture, medicine, defense.

### Post-Architect Era (Era 8, WC 83–91 onward)

Magic and science merge.

### Mira Era (Movie 2–3, WC ≈182–192)

Civilization can manipulate space-time and observe timeline branches; the truth of the Builders becomes accessible to a rare few.

## The Architect's City — renamed "Elena" (RESOLVED, v8)

Birthplace and childhood home of The Architect; also where he first meets Elena (WC15). Pre-rename, the city's leader was a close personal friend of the Architect's father — which is why the Architect's early inventions were permitted for civic use ahead of formal recognition. City Project formally begins WC28.

**The "Architect's City" nickname (new, v8).** As the city's reputation spreads during the nation-building era (~WC28–31), citizens, nobles, and foreign visitors begin informally calling it "the Architect's City." He privately dislikes this — it runs directly against his own stated principle, _"I don't want to become a god"_ (`21-dialogue-memory.md`), and against his refusal to let his influence calcify into a personality cult. He never issues a formal correction; he simply never uses the nickname himself and quietly redirects it when others do. This should be shown through behavior, not stated outright (see `10-light-novel-writing-guide.md`).

Heavily destroyed during the Great War (WC35); rebuilt by the Architect afterward. He personally renames it **"Elena"** after her death — the city itself becomes a monument to her, reinforcing the saga's "Home" motif (see `43-ost-reference.md` Tracks 07/08). This is not only memorial: it is also his final, deliberate refusal of the "Architect's City" framing people kept trying to hand him — at the one moment everyone expects him to finally accept a title built on his own name, he gives the name to her instead.

Political form: an independent city-state, not a monarchy — the Architect never claims rulership; his influence is technological/economic (consistent with "I don't want to become a god," `21-dialogue-memory.md`).

**Final stylization (RESOLVED, v8): plain "Elena."** No derived or stylized form. This was previously OPEN; it is now settled canon. Do not reintroduce an alternate stylization without explicit owner instruction.

Growth comes from reliable infrastructure, safe trade, accessible magic, improved food production, migration, stable institutions.

## Home

Symbolic evolution:

physical shelter → Elena → family → city → civilization → freedom

## Historical Memory

Series (Season 1): The Architect is alive.

Movie 1: he becomes, then dies as, a deeply contested/understood historical figure to his own son.

Movie 2: legend, ~100 years gone.

Movie 3: legend under active historical revision, ~110 years gone.

Movie 4: nearly forgotten, ~1,000 years gone.

The erosion of historical memory is intentional.
