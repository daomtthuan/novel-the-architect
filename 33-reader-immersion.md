# Reader Immersion Specification (v6, World Calendar canon)

## Core Objective

The reader should experience Season 1 as if they themselves were transported into the world — reborn, in fact, since the Architect's own arrival is now a birth (WC0), not an adult awakening.

## Protagonist Naming

The protagonist has no personal name. Do not invent one. "The Architect" is a later title, earned WC35.

## Pronouns

Season 1: first-person "I / me / my," from birth onward.

Other characters should normally address him naturally without repeatedly using a name.

Examples:

- "Are you coming?"
- "You really built this?"
- "Architect." only after the title is established (WC35 onward).

## Physical Description

Use enough detail to establish clothing, posture, movement, physical condition. Avoid unnecessary facial specification. In early childhood chapters, physical description should read as genuinely childlike (small hands, a child's balance and stamina) even while the internal voice reads as adult — the contrast is a feature, not an error.

## Strong Characterization

Self-insert compatibility does not mean weak characterization. The protagonist still has intelligence, humor, stubbornness, values, flaws, emotional wounds, preferences, technical habits — carried over from his adult life on Earth, expressed through a growing child's, then a man's, body.

## Interior Experience

Prioritize internal reactions.

Instead of:

> The city was beautiful.

Prefer:

> I had seen cities before, in another life, with other eyes. None of them had ever felt like something I built with these hands.

## Elena, Iris, Seraphine, Isolde

Elena is the primary emotional anchor; the reader should feel she is a complete person, not an NPC. Iris, Seraphine, and Isolde should each read as complete people with their own reasons for being present, not as rewards.

## Later Installments

The reader is intentionally moved away from direct protagonist embodiment.

Movie 1: the Son asks what it means to be the Architect's child, having been raised by his father and by Iris and Seraphine together.

Movie 2: Mira studies him as history.

Movie 3: The Other Builder becomes an uncomfortable mirror.

Movie 4: Noah demonstrates that the future belongs to ordinary people, not to Builders.

## Visual Immersion

Images of the protagonist should support reader projection. Use environmental and cinematic compositions more often than portrait cards. The world should remain the visual focus as often as the protagonist.
