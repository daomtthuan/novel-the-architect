# Romance Integration by Installment (v6, World Calendar canon)

## Season 1 (Series) — Romance as Life

Romance is most visible here.

The relationship with Elena should grow alongside: childhood and magic; The Teacher and his loss; settlement; technology; politics; Isolde's proposal; war.

Iris and Seraphine both appear naturally through the main plot — Iris from the very start (WC15, platonic), Seraphine from the war build-up (WC33). Their presence should make the world larger, not distract from Elena.

### Recommended Balance
Main plot: 65–75%
Romance / slice of life: 20–30%
Fan-service: occasional layer inside the above

## Movie 1 — Romance as Found Family

The Son sees his father's grief, and sees two women — Iris and Seraphine — who are not his mother but who raised him anyway.

Fan-service is minimal; warmth and found-family dynamics replace it.

The important romantic question, seen from the Son's POV, becomes:

> Why did none of them ever become my second mother — and what does that say about how much my father loved my first one?

## Movie 2 — Romance as Historical Evidence

Mira investigates the past. She encounters old records, contradictory accounts, and — crucially — the first hints of Elena's true nature as a Quantum Anchor.

Romance is used to reconstruct history, not to romance Mira herself in a primary way.

## Movie 3 — Romance as Contrast

The Other Builder's alternate Elena allows the story to ask:

> What happens when a version of the same love story refuses to let the other person choose?

This should be emotionally heavy. Fan-service is minimal.

## Movie 4 — Romance as Legacy

The world has moved on. Romantic elements become part of ordinary life.

The central theme is:

> The Architect built a world where future generations could choose whom to love and how to live — and refused, himself, to take more than one such choice for his own.

Fan-service is occasional and light.
