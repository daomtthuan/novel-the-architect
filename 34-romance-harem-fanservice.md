# Romance, Harem & Fan-Service System (v6, World Calendar canon)

## Purpose

This file controls romantic subplots, light harem elements, and tasteful fan-service.

These elements must be **subordinate to the main story**.

The central romance remains:

**The Architect × Elena**

Elena is the primary heroine and emotional home of the protagonist.

## Core Rule

Iris, Seraphine, and Isolde may:

- develop and hold genuine attraction;
- flirt or tease (Seraphine especially, early on);
- become emotionally attached;
- experience or trigger jealousy (Elena's jealousy of Seraphine, WC33, is a named canon event);
- create romantic misunderstandings;
- have real chemistry with the protagonist.

However, the story must not casually turn The Architect into a man with multiple wives. He is offered the chance more than once (Isolde's proposal; Elena's own later consent to a second wife) and refuses every time. The harem element is a **secondary flavor**, not the protagonist's defining trait, and his refusals are as characterful as his love for Elena.

## Character Age Rule (hard constraint)

All romantic and fan-service characters must be clearly established as adults **at the time of any romantic or fan-service framing**.

Iris specifically: she is introduced at WC15 at age 12, as a strictly platonic friend of Elena and the Architect. Any attraction on her part is not written, shown, or referenced until she is an adult, placed at ~WC21 or later. This is a deliberate, permanent continuity correction and must never be reversed, regardless of what a source spreadsheet cell implies about earlier timing.

Never introduce romantic or sexualized framing for minors, for any character, under any circumstance.

## Heroine Hierarchy

| Character | Romantic Role                                 | Intensity | Resolution                                    |
| --------- | --------------------------------------------- | --------: | --------------------------------------------- |
| Elena     | Primary heroine / wife                        |       5/5 | Married; dies WC35                            |
| Iris      | Secondary — quiet, unspoken                   |       3/5 | Self-withdrawal; permanent found-family       |
| Seraphine | Secondary — guilt-driven, offered and refused |       3/5 | Permanently refused; co-parent                |
| Isolde    | Political-then-genuine                        |       3/5 | Refused twice (WC26 and later); enduring ally |

These values are narrative guidance, not a requirement that every character actively pursue him in every chapter.

## Integration Principle

Every romantic subplot must do at least one useful job:

1. reveal character;
2. create political complications (Isolde);
3. reveal cultural/racial differences (Seraphine, a Succubus; Iris, purely Human);
4. provide emotional contrast;
5. provide comedy (especially Seraphine);
6. deepen Elena's characterization (Iris's loyalty to Elena; Elena's jealousy of Seraphine);
7. foreshadow a later conflict;
8. expose something about The Architect's values (his repeated refusals).

If a romantic scene does none of these, remove or shorten it.

## Fan-Service Frequency

### Season 1 (Series)

High relative frequency, but still secondary to plot.

### Movie 1

Low. Centered on found-family warmth (Iris/Seraphine raising the Son) rather than romance.

### Movie 2

Minimal.

### Movie 3

Light and character-oriented, where relevant to The Other Builder's alternate-Elena material — handled with care, not as spectacle.

### Movie 4

Very low. Prioritize legacy and the future.

## Fan-Service Tone

Allowed: teasing; playful jealousy; beach/festival clothing; changing-room comedy; accidental proximity; awkward situations; romantic tension; affectionate domestic moments; character-focused illustrations; comedic misunderstandings.

Keep presentation tasteful and non-graphic. Seraphine's Succubus nature and her post-WC35 "offers of comfort" must always be written as _refused_ — never fulfilled, never explicit.

## Elena Protection Rule

Never use another heroine to make Elena look inferior. Never make Elena stupid, oblivious, disposable, or replaceable.

The existence of Iris, Seraphine, and Isolde should often reinforce how strong Elena's relationship with him actually is — and, after her death, how singular she remains even to three women who each, in their own way, genuinely love him.
