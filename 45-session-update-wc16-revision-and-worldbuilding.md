## 3. The Architect's City → Renamed "Elena" (add to `05-world.md`, `22-location-registry.md`)

- Birthplace and childhood home of The Architect; also where he first meets Elena (WC15).
- Heavily destroyed during the Great War (WC35).
- Rebuilt by the Architect after the war.
- **He personally renames it "Elena"** after her death — the city itself becomes a monument to her, reinforcing the saga's "Home" motif (`43-ost-reference.md` Tracks 07/08).
- Political form unchanged from the earlier session decision: independent city-state, not a monarchy — the Architect never claims rulership; his influence is technological/economic (consistent with "I don't want to become a god," `21-dialogue-memory.md`).
- Pre-rename, the city's leader was a close personal friend of the Architect's father, which is why the Architect's early inventions were permitted for civic use ahead of formal recognition.

## 4. MAJOR REVISION — WC16 "First Major Loss"

Supersedes the current version in: `00-story-bible.md`, `01-canon-rules.md`, `02-timeline.md`, `03-characters.md`, `04-relationships.md`, `14-character-psychology.md`, `15-character-voice.md`, `18-event-consequence-map.md`, `19-emotional-memory.md`, `20-scene-memory.md`.

**New backstory:**

The Architect's biological parents in this world were both members of a 5-person A-rank adventuring party:
1. **Father** — swordsman.
2. **Mother** — healer / enchantress.
3. **A-rank mage** — later becomes **The Teacher**.
4. **Tanker** — later becomes **Guild Master**.
5. **Scout / investigator-assassin**.

- Parents retire from adventuring after marrying; the party disbands.
- Member 3 (mage) goes on to teach independently; at the father's request, he later takes on The Architect as a student (WC9–10, unchanged from prior canon).
- Member 4 continues adventuring and rises to Guild Master.
- Member 5 continues as an investigative adventurer.
- ~10 years later (WC16), Member 5 uncovers signs of unusual Demon activity — the earliest seed of what will eventually escalate into the Great War. The old party reunites (minus Member 4, now Guild Master, who stays back coordinating the guild's response) for a defensive operation, bringing the 16-year-old Architect along for his first real combat experience.
- The engagement unexpectedly escalates into a fight against a **high-tier Demon**, far stronger than anticipated.
- The Architect deduces what he believes is the demon's weakness — but this is overconfidence: the demon deliberately baits the party, exploiting his tactical read.
- The Architect is badly wounded; the rest of the party is forced into an all-out defense to save him, absorbing the demon's finishing blow meant for him.
- His father physically throws him clear at the last possible moment.
- **Father, mother, the Teacher, and the scout all die. The Architect is the sole survivor.** The Guild Master (Member 4) was not present and survives, later supporting the Architect materially and politically through the Guild.

**Psychological continuity note:** this does not contradict existing Darkest Point psychology — it grounds it. The Architect's established flaw "over-optimization / arrogance during success" (`03-characters.md`) is now the literal cause of the ambush: he found a real-seeming tactical solution and was deceived. The existing signature line "If I had modeled one more variable, he would still be alive" (`15-character-voice.md`) now has a concrete mechanism behind it.

**OPEN:** the high-tier Demon's fate/role after WC16. Three options were proposed, undecided:
(a) dies/is critically wounded in the fight, no longer a direct threat;
(b) escapes and becomes a slow-burn threat, possibly resurfacing at or before the Great War (WC35);
(c) tied directly to Seraphine's domination-seeking faction, creating a WC16 → WC33–35 throughline.
