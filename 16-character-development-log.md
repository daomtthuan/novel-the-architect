# Character Development Log (v8, World Calendar canon)

## The Architect

WC0–8: adult mind, infant body — curiosity and quiet strangeness.
WC9–15: prodigy, confidence, ambition, under The Teacher.
**WC16: First Major Loss, fully dramatized (Chapter 11).** The field party clears minor/mid-tier Demons easily, then a high-tier Demon appears. The Architect misreads a deliberately-baited "weakness" in its combat pattern; the Scout dies exploiting the false opening. Attempting an untested high-tier spell, he is rushed by the Demon during the casting window; Mother's shield and The Teacher's layered barrier both fail; Father shoves him clear at the last instant, and Mother, Father, and The Teacher all die together absorbing the finishing blow, Father's body striking him with a fatal wound visible. In desperation, the Architect breaks The Teacher's core rule against channeling Mana through his own body, unleashing a self-designed energy-compression spell that gravely wounds the Demon (which flees) at the permanent cost of his own left arm. He is the sole survivor, found unconscious by a rescue party led by Elena and Iris.
**WC16, immediate aftermath (Chapter 12):** wakes after six days unconscious, to find Elena has cared for him throughout and moves temporarily into his home. Attends the funeral of Father, Mother, and The Teacher (three graves under an old oak; the Scout is buried separately in her hometown) without visible grief, instead beginning to compulsively calculate what could have prevented each death — the first on-page symptom of the coping mechanism that becomes the Darkest Point, directly named and gently challenged by Elena. Recovers Father's sword and The Teacher's unfinished research notebook. Attempts one-handed spellcasting for the first time and discovers his established two-handed Mana-channeling technique no longer functions — a concrete technical loss mirroring the emotional one. Closes the chapter with a private, dangerous internal vow ("if I understand enough, I will never lose anyone again") that becomes the explicit seed of the Darkest Point ideology.
WC16–23: the Darkest Point — control crisis, now grounded in a concrete, on-page catastrophic failure and a specific, named psychological turning point (Chapter 12) rather than an abstract flaw.
WC23–24: breakthrough, marriage.
WC25–34: nation-builder, husband, wartime leader-in-waiting.
WC35: loss and acceptance — becomes "The Architect."
WC36–82: legacy and fatherhood.
Post-WC82: becomes historical memory (Movie 1 onward).

**Permanent physical marker (v7, unchanged):** missing left arm from WC16 onward — see `30-visual-bible.md`, `03-characters.md`.

## Elena

WC0–14 (pre-story): independent adult of the Eterna people.
WC15–23: companion, then the one who breaks his ideology. **WC16 (Chapter 11):** senses the battle's disturbance from a distance too late to intervene directly; organizes and joins the rescue party with Iris; is among the first to find the Architect alive. **WC16, immediate aftermath (Chapter 12):** cares for the unconscious Architect for six days, then moves temporarily into his home during his recovery; is the first to directly name his shift toward calculation instead of grief, offering an early, imperfect version of the lesson she will fully deliver at WC23 ("not everything can be calculated away").
WC24–34: partner and co-builder, mother-to-be.
WC35: chooses sacrifice.
Post-WC35: becomes emotional and metaphysical anchor (Quantum Anchor, revealed Movie 2–3).

## Iris

WC3–14: childhood, growing friendship with Elena.
WC15: introduced alongside Elena, strictly platonic (age 12).
**WC16 (Chapter 11, age 13):** helps mobilize the rescue party alongside Elena via the village elder and the Guild Master; present at the discovery of the Architect, sole survivor.
**WC16, immediate aftermath (Chapter 12, age 13):** continues caretaking in small, practical, wordless ways (mending, reorganizing the house for one-handed use); when directly asked, briefly admits her own grief, stating she will allow herself to feel it fully only once the Architect no longer needs her — establishing a durable pattern of deferred self-care that recurs through her arc.
WC21–24: adult, unspoken feelings, self-withdrawal at the marriage.
WC35 onward: co-parent, sword teacher, permanent found-family.

## The Architect's Father (deceased, WC16)

WC0–15: retired A-rank swordsman, loving father, quiet guardian.
**WC16 (Chapter 11): dies protecting the Architect — physically shoves him clear of the Demon's finishing blow at the last possible instant, then is killed alongside Mother and The Teacher absorbing that same blow. His body strikes the Architect immediately after, revealing the fatal wound.**
**WC16, aftermath (Chapter 12):** buried in the village cemetery under an old oak, alongside Mother and The Teacher. His sword, kept within reach by the door out of old habit, is recovered by the Architect as a personal artifact (see `24-artifact-registry.md`).

## The Architect's Mother (deceased, WC16)

WC0–15: retired A-rank healer/enchantress, loving mother, quiet guardian; carries the emotional intelligence in the household, the one who first taught the Architect that magic "always has its price."
**WC16 (Chapter 11): dies shielding the Architect with the strongest light-magic barrier of her life, layered together with The Teacher's barrier — both fail against the Demon's finishing blow. Dies alongside Father and The Teacher in the same instant, absorbing the attack meant for her son.**
**WC16, aftermath (Chapter 12):** buried in the village cemetery under an old oak, alongside Father and The Teacher.

## The Teacher (deceased, WC16)

WC9: discovers and accepts the Architect as an exceptional, guarded exception to his refusal to take students.
WC10–15: formal mentor; establishes the core teaching principle — draw Mana from the environment, never from your own body — and repeatedly warns against untested confidence.
**WC16 (Chapter 11): rejoins the reunited five-person party for the defensive operation; in the final battle, layers a binding/barrier spell over Mother's shield in a last attempt to protect the Architect. The combined barrier fails. Dies alongside Father and Mother, absorbing the Demon's finishing blow.**
**WC16, aftermath (Chapter 12):** buried in the village cemetery under an old oak, alongside Father and Mother; attended by several of his former students, now grown, from neighboring towns. His unfinished research notebook is recovered by the Architect from his abandoned house — contents unread/unexplored as of Chapter 12.
His own unresolved mysteries — the interrupted remark about "someone who thought this way" (Chapter 4), his sharp, unexplained attentiveness toward Elena (Chapter 7) — are now permanently unresolvable from his own mouth. Chapter 12 confirms the Guild Master knows The Teacher had "his own reasons" for refusing students for years, but explicitly declines to share them — closed information channels unless another character is later shown to have known something (agent's discretion, not to be invented casually).

## The Scout / Investigator (deceased, WC16)

WC16 (Chapter 9): uncovers the earliest signs of unusual Demon activity in the eastern forest, the discovery that reunites the old party.
**WC16 (Chapter 11): dies first in the final battle, exploiting a false "weakness" the Architect identified in the Demon's combat pattern — a deliberate bait. Killed by the Demon's previously unused right arm. Her death is the first, direct, undeniable cost of the Architect's overconfidence, occurring before any of the other three deaths.**
**WC16, aftermath (Chapter 12):** per her own prior wishes, her body is returned to and buried in her own hometown, separate from the three graves in the Architect's home village.

## The Guild Master

WC16 (Chapter 9): introduced at Guild headquarters; absent from the WC16 battle by design, remaining behind to coordinate the guild's response.
**WC16 (Chapter 11): authorizes and dispatches the rescue party that ultimately finds the Architect alive.**
**WC16, aftermath (Chapter 12):** attends the funeral and speaks privately with the Architect afterward — expresses guilt for not being present at the battle, shares personal memories of the old five-person party (their individual strengths, why they retired), confirms without detail that The Teacher had private reasons for refusing students for years, and offers ongoing Guild support without pressuring the Architect toward any immediate decision.
Sole adult survivor of the original five-person party; becomes a recurring political and material support figure for the Architect from WC16 onward, through The Guild.
