# Character Development Log (v9, World Calendar canon)

## The Architect

WC0–8: adult mind, infant body — curiosity and quiet strangeness.
WC9–15: prodigy, confidence, ambition, under The Teacher.
**WC16: First Major Loss, fully dramatized (Chapter 11).** The field party clears minor/mid-tier Demons easily, then a high-tier Demon appears. The Architect misreads a deliberately-baited "weakness" in its combat pattern; the Scout dies exploiting the false opening. Attempting an untested high-tier spell, he is rushed by the Demon during the casting window; Mother's shield and The Teacher's layered barrier both fail; Father shoves him clear at the last instant, and Mother, Father, and The Teacher all die together absorbing the finishing blow. In desperation, the Architect breaks The Teacher's core rule against channeling Mana through his own body, unleashing a self-designed energy-compression spell that gravely wounds the Demon (which flees) at the permanent cost of his own left arm. He is the sole survivor, found unconscious by a rescue party led by Elena and Iris.
**WC16, immediate aftermath (Chapter 12):** wakes after six days unconscious, to find Elena has cared for him throughout and moves temporarily into his home. Attends the funeral without visible grief, instead beginning to compulsively calculate what could have prevented each death — the first on-page symptom of the coping mechanism that becomes the Darkest Point, directly named and gently challenged by Elena. Recovers Father's sword and The Teacher's unfinished research notebook. Discovers his established two-handed Mana-channeling technique no longer functions. Closes the chapter with a private, dangerous internal vow ("if I understand enough, I will never lose anyone again") that becomes the explicit seed of the Darkest Point ideology.
**WC17, one year later (Chapter 13):** the "first crack" from Chapter 12 calcifies into a full year of systematic avoidance behavior. Develops a near-instant-cast spellcasting technique by compressing the standard casting sequence into reflex, drawing on Earth-origin physics/chemistry/biology concepts. Realizes that his own body — not the spell's backlash — was the true point of failure in his WC16 arm loss, and conceives the earliest, unnamed technical seed of an externalized Mana pathway (a direct, deliberate precursor to later Ark technology, inspired by Earth-origin gun-barrel and rocket-engine concepts). Carves pre-woven spell structures onto Father's sword as a makeshift storage/release method. Skips meals, gives curt answers, and isolates himself in his research space; privately finds this isolation comforting rather than distressing. Elena directly names this pattern as systemic, not momentary, escalating her Chapter 12 concern into open confrontation. Registers as an independent Guild adventurer, taking low-tier solo missions specifically to avoid ever again needing anyone else to die protecting him. Begins compiling fragmentary, HINTED-level rumors of unusual Demon activity during these missions. The village's collective attitude toward him shifts subtly toward awe and distance rather than warmth.
WC16–23: the Darkest Point — control crisis, now grounded in a concrete, on-page catastrophic failure and a specific, named psychological turning point (Chapter 12), escalating through a full year of systematized avoidance (Chapter 13), toward the WC18 major argument.
WC23–24: breakthrough, marriage.
WC25–34: nation-builder, husband, wartime leader-in-waiting.
WC35: loss and acceptance — becomes "The Architect."
WC36–82: legacy and fatherhood.
Post-WC82: becomes historical memory (Movie 1 onward).

**Permanent physical marker (unchanged):** missing left arm from WC16 onward — see `30-visual-bible.md`, `03-characters.md`.

## Elena

WC0–14 (pre-story): independent adult of the Eterna people.
WC15–23: companion, then the one who breaks his ideology. **WC16 (Chapter 11):** senses the battle's disturbance from a distance too late to intervene directly; organizes and joins the rescue party with Iris; is among the first to find the Architect alive. **WC16, immediate aftermath (Chapter 12):** cares for the unconscious Architect for six days, then moves temporarily into his home during his recovery; is the first to directly name his shift toward calculation instead of grief, offering an early, imperfect version of the lesson she will fully deliver at WC23. **WC17 (Chapter 13):** continues living in/near his home; escalates her Chapter 12 concern into direct confrontation, explicitly naming his year-long withdrawal as a systemic pattern rather than a passing moment — the direct emotional precursor to the WC18 major argument.
WC24–34: partner and co-builder, mother-to-be.
WC35: chooses sacrifice.
Post-WC35: becomes emotional and metaphysical anchor (Quantum Anchor, revealed Movie 2–3).

## Iris

**WC16, immediate aftermath (Chapter 12, age 13):** continues caretaking in small, practical, wordless ways; briefly admits her own grief when directly asked, stating she will allow herself to feel it fully only once the Architect no longer needs her.
**WC17 (Chapter 13, age 14):** continues indirect caretaking (bringing food, small practical gestures); begins to consciously register the Architect's withdrawal as a pattern rather than a passing grief response — a quiet but important shift in her own perceptiveness. Still strictly platonic.
