# Light Novel Writing Guide (v6, World Calendar canon)

## Narrative Style

Use polished, cinematic prose. Prioritize psychology, atmosphere, subtext, sensory detail, meaningful dialogue, world-building through action.

## POV

Respect installment-specific narration.

Season 1 (Series): The Architect, first-person, from birth.

Movie 1: The Son, first-person, framed by his father's death.

Movie 2: Mira, first-person.

Movie 3: The Other Builder and Mira, dual first-person.

Movie 4: Noah, first-person. Final scene may briefly switch to third-person.

## Voice Principle

POV is not just a grammatical choice. The reader should perceive reality differently depending on who narrates.

The Architect notices systems, and — in early chapters — occasionally notices how strange it is to think this clearly in a child's body.

The Son notices his father's humanity.

Mira notices inconsistencies.

The Other Builder notices optimization.

Noah notices ordinary life.

## Internal Monologue

The Architect should think technically but not like a documentation generator. His technical reasoning should contrast with emotional problems that cannot be optimized — especially in the Darkest Point era (WC16–23), where the contrast IS the tragedy.

## Dialogue

Use subtext, pauses, gestures, changes in tone, silence. Avoid constant exposition.

## Romance

Build intimacy through meals, walking, working together, arguments, jokes, quiet nights, shared projects. The reader must understand why the Architect and Elena love each other before she dies.

## Light Harem / Fanservice

Four named heroines: Elena (primary), Iris, Seraphine, Isolde.

Intended feeling:

> Several women may be genuinely attracted to the protagonist, but the reader always knows Elena is the heroine — and that his refusals (of Isolde, of Seraphine) are as much a part of his character as his love for Elena.

Secondary heroines can provide comedy, jealousy, political complications, friendship, rivalry, admiration.

Do not make every woman instantly fall in love with him. Iris's feelings must never be written or referenced while she is a minor (see `01-canon-rules.md`, Character Age Rule).

Season 1 contains the most fanservice. After Elena's death, romantic fanservice involving the Architect becomes extremely restrained — Seraphine's visits are written as offers he declines.

## Emotional Scenes

Slow down. Use: shock, denial, silence, physical detail, realization, consequence.

## Action

Action must change character or plot. Avoid spectacle without consequence. The Teacher's death (WC16) is the template: a battlefield mistake with permanent psychological consequence, not a spectacle beat.

## Humor

Use during peaceful periods. Do not insert jokes into Elena's death scene, The Teacher's death scene, or the Architect's own death scene.

## Recurring Motifs

lights · architecture · doors · windows · empty rooms · home · the difference between "building" and "controlling"

## Chapter Endings

Prefer emotional turn, new question, discovery, irreversible decision.

## Protagonist Self-Insert Rule

The protagonist has no personal name. Never invent one. "The Architect" is not a personal name, and is not interchangeable with "Builder."

### Season 1 Language

Use first-person from birth: I, me, my. Avoid naming the protagonist in dialogue unless a title is being intentionally bestowed. Handle the infant-body/adult-mind premise with restraint — establish it early, then let it recede into normal first-person narration rather than repeating the gimmick every scene.

### Characterization Without Naming

Give the protagonist a strong identity through thought patterns, decisions, technical reasoning, humor, fears, values, mistakes, relationships. Self-insert compatibility does NOT mean bland.
