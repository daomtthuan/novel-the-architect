# Canon Rules (v6, World Calendar canon)

## Character Rules

- The Architect is reborn as an infant at WC0 but retains full adult memory/reasoning from birth; he is brilliant but not omniscient, and his adult mind does not make him emotionally mature — he still has a childhood, a mentor, and a corruption arc to go through.
- He is capable of serious mistakes — his First Major Loss (The Teacher's death, WC16) is a direct result of his own tactical miscalculation.
- His technical mindset is both strength and weakness.
- He can become controlling when afraid; this is not a minor quirk but a full "Darkest Point" era (WC16–23) during which he treats people as optimizable variables.
- Elena has independent goals and agency. Her agency is the thematic hinge of the entire saga.
- Elena is never merely a sacrifice device.
- Iris and Seraphine have their own independent arcs, flaws, and motives — they exist for reasons beyond attraction to the protagonist.
- The Son is not a copy of his father.
- Mira is not a blood descendant of The Architect.
- Noah is not biologically related to The Architect.
- The Other Builder is a genuine alternate version of the protagonist, not a cartoon villain — and is specifically defined by never becoming an Architect.

## Elena Rules

- Elena belongs to the Eterna, a long-lived Light/Time-affinity people; she ages at 0.25× the rate of a Human (see aging table, `00-story-bible.md`).
- Elena is already an adult (physically ~24) when she meets the Architect (who is 15 in body / adult in mind) at WC15.
- Primary Elena dies during The Ark activation, at WC35, the same day their son is born.
- She voluntarily chooses to become its core.
- The Architect cannot secretly save her.
- She is not cheaply resurrected.
- Alternate Elenas (other timelines) do not erase primary Elena.
- Her death permanently changes The Architect, and it is his acceptance of her choice — not the act of activating the Ark itself — that later comes to define the title "Architect."

## Great War Choice

A. Activate The Ark: millions survive, Elena dies (by her own choice).

B. Refuse The Ark: Elena survives, millions die.

Do not invent a convenient third solution after the dilemma is established. **The Other Builder is the "what if he chose to save her instead" answer, explored fully in Movie 3 — it is not a solution the primary Architect ever gets to take.**

## Power Rules

Do not turn the story into endless power escalation.

The Architect's strongest abilities are:

- systems thinking (an adult mind, applied to magic from early childhood);
- engineering;
- infrastructure;
- strategy;
- leadership;
- learning.

## Romance / Harem / Fanservice

Light harem elements are permitted, centered on four women: Elena (primary), Iris, Seraphine, and Isolde.

Other female characters can have:

- attraction;
- chemistry;
- flirtation;
- jealousy;
- romantic tension.

But:

- Elena is the sole primary heroine;
- no secondary heroine replaces her;
- no multi-wife ending — the Architect refuses Isolde's political marriage even when Elena herself is willing to allow it, and refuses Seraphine's post-war comfort;
- no romantic collection fantasy.

### Character Age Rule (hard constraint — overrides raw Calendar cell timing)

All romantic or fanservice framing must involve characters who are clearly adults at the time of that framing. Iris is introduced at WC15 as a close platonic friend (she is 12 at that point) — any romantic feeling on her part is **not depicted or referenced until she is an adult**, placed narratively around WC21–24 (Iris age 18–21), coinciding with the Architect and Elena's relationship deepening toward marriage. This is a deliberate continuity correction to the raw spreadsheet timing and must not be reversed.

Fanservice can include:

- playful teasing;
- embarrassing situations;
- festivals;
- beach or bathhouse comedy;
- attractive character presentation;
- light ecchi humor.

Season 1 has the most. Fanservice decreases as the story becomes darker.

After Elena's death, romantic fanservice involving The Architect should be extremely restrained — Seraphine's post-war visits are written as _offers he refuses_, never as fulfilled romance.

## POV Rules

### Season 1 (Series)

The Architect — first-person, from birth.

### Movie 1

The Son — first-person, framed by his father's death at the opening/closing.

### Movie 2

Mira — first-person.

### Movie 3

The Other Builder and Mira — dual first-person. Do not switch POV inside a scene.

### Movie 4

Noah — first-person. Final scene may briefly use third-person.

## Lore Rules

Quantum, multiverse, and Builder truths must be revealed gradually.

Season 1 and Movie 1 may contain foreshadowing but should not openly explain the Builder mechanics. Full explanation is reserved for Movies 2–3.

"Builder" and "Architect" are not synonyms — see `08-multiverse-and-quantum-lore.md`.

## Ending Rules

- No Movie 5 / no Season 6.
- Do not reset reality into one correct timeline.
- Preserve free will.
- The Architect's legacy survives even if his name fades.
- Final emotional concept: Home.
