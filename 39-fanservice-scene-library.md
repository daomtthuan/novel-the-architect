# Fan-Service Scene Library (v6, World Calendar canon)

## Purpose

Provide the AI agent with reusable patterns without forcing them into every chapter.

All characters in romantic / fan-service scenes are adults at the time of the scene. Iris specifically must never appear in this library's scene types before ~WC21.

## Type A — Teasing

Seraphine (especially) intentionally embarrasses The Architect. Purpose: establish personality; create chemistry; provide comic relief.

## Type B — Jealousy

Elena notices Seraphine's flirting (WC33) or a similar harmless-seeming interaction. Purpose: reveal relationships; create dialogue; strengthen Elena's confidence rather than undermine it.

## Type C — Travel

Characters share carriage, inn, camp, boat, or tent-area proximity. Use proximity for conversation and comedy. Do not turn every travel scene into sexual content.

## Type D — Festival

Costumes, food, games, dancing, fireworks, romantic atmosphere. One of the best places for illustrations — Isolde arc benefits especially from formal festival/reception settings.

## Type E — Beach / Summer

Tasteful summer outfits and playful situations. Focus on character personality, comedy, relationship dynamics.

## Type F — Training

Combat or magic training can create accidental proximity, teasing, competitive tension, mutual respect. Iris's swordwork with the Son (post-WC42) and Seraphine's magic lessons (post-WC48) are the primary Movie 1 use of this type — reframed as mentorship, not romance.

## Type G — Domestic

Especially for Elena: cooking, cleaning, waking up, choosing clothes, decorating the home, discussing the future child. Domestic romance is more important than sexual spectacle. Post-WC35, this type shifts to Iris/Seraphine's domestic caretaking of the Son.

## Type H — Aftermath

After a battle, a character notices another person's injuries. A small gesture can become more intimate than an explicit scene. Seraphine's post-WC35 "offers of comfort" belong here — always as an offer, never as fulfillment.

## Hard Rule

If the scene can be removed without changing character, relationship, mood, world, or plot, then it is probably unnecessary.
