## 2. City Name — RESOLVED

**Final stylization: plain "Elena."** No derived form. This closes the OPEN item in `05-world.md`, `22-location-registry.md`, `28-unresolved-threads.md`, and `45-session-update-wc16-revision-and-worldbuilding.md`.

**New motivation layer (confirmed this session):**

During the nation-building era (~WC28–31), as the city's reputation spreads, common usage — citizens, visiting nobles, foreign dignitaries — starts informally calling it **"the Architect's City."** He privately dislikes this. It sits directly against his established core line, *"I don't want to become a god"* (`21-dialogue-memory.md` / `46-iconic-dialogue-registry.md` §1.1), and against his broader refusal to let his influence calcify into a personality cult. He never issues a formal correction at this stage — he simply never uses the nickname himself, and quietly redirects it when others do (show, don't state, per `10-light-novel-writing-guide.md`).

This sets up the real payoff: after the WC35 destruction and postwar rebuilding, the moment everyone expects him to finally accept the "Architect's City" title for good, he instead gives the name to her. Renaming it "Elena" isn't only memorial — it's also a final, deliberate refusal of the god-title people kept trying to hand him.

**Suggested new scene** (optional, P1): a foreign dignitary calls it "the Architect's City" sometime WC29–31; he deflects with a line about what the city is *for*, not who built it. Good candidate for `20-scene-memory.md` and a future illustration entry.

## 3. High-Tier Demon (WC16) — Fate Resolved

Resolved creatively per your instruction, without re-asking. Summary:

**Identity.** Retroactively an ancient demon lord, believed destroyed or sealed generations before WC0 in an unspecified prior conflict ("the Sealing" — left as ambient deep-history, not explained, consistent with how `26-mystery-board.md` layers reveals). Referred to in continuity notes only as **"The Unburied One"** — an epithet, not a true name. The true name is intentionally never given; this deliberately echoes the protagonist's own namelessness rather than being an oversight, and can be used as a quiet structural rhyme later if you like it.

**WC16.** The Scout's "signs of unusual Demon activity" (`45-session-update-wc16-revision-and-worldbuilding.md` §4) were early evidence the old seal was failing. The battle critically wounds the Unburied One — closer to option (a) than a clean kill — forcing it into long dormancy rather than killing it outright.

**WC16–32.** Dormant and recovering. It takes no direct action, but its patient, indirect influence — old cultist networks, proxies — quietly nudges parts of Demon society toward the domination-seeking ideology, never surfacing publicly. This does **not** retcon Seraphine's faction leadership into puppets; they remain a self-directed political faction (`06-factions-and-politics.md`) with their own real agenda. The Unburied One is a hidden patron behind them, not their commander — and Seraphine herself never learns this connection exists. Her arc, agency, and defection are untouched.

**WC32–35.** As Demon activity intensifies and Seraphine is dispatched (WC33), the Unburied One is one of the unseen reasons that faction leadership felt emboldened enough to escalate all the way to the Great War. This gives the WC16 → WC33–35 throughline (your option c) without complicating Seraphine's own story.

**Post-WC35.** Presumed destroyed when Demon territories collapse (WC40, `world_calendar.md`) during the Great War's turning point — but no confirmed remains. Left as a genuine loose thread: status **PARTIALLY RESOLVED**, with the exact ending available either for a future reveal (Movie 1 historical footnote, etc.) or to remain **INTENTIONALLY UNANSWERED** (a valid status per `28-unresolved-threads.md`'s own legend).
